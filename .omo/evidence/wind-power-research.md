# Wind-driven turbine estimate: evidence and implementation boundary (2026-09-30)

## Local facts

- `renderers/twin/spec.json` records Tamra operator specifications of **3 MW per turbine** and **91.59 m rotor diameter**, sourced to [Tamra operator specifications](http://tamra-owp.co.kr/2019/sub0204.php). The same file says the correspondence of those specifications to each GIS object is tentative; its 80 m hub height is a visualization estimate. `docs/estimated-twin.md` identifies the ten displayed wind objects as Tamra IDs 5722–5731. These are **not** the separate Hankyung Vestas machines.
- `src/weather.rs` and `docs/estimated-twin.md` identify the current input as nearby KMA ground-station **10-minute mean** wind speed in m/s and 16-point **wind-from** direction, with up to 43 Jeju stations. The two western turbines currently select Gosan, the other eight Nackcheon. There is no hub/nacelle anemometer, turbine SCADA, observed rotor RPM, availability, curtailment, or individual generation telemetry. `renderers/twin/app.js` presently rotates at an expressly demonstrative 4 RPM.

## Primary technical sources

- [US DOE wind guidebook](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/systems/windexchange/small-wind-guidebook): wind power relationship `P = ½ ρ A Cp v³`, `A = πD²/4`; `Cp` is turbine-dependent and limited by Betz; cut-in and cut-out describe **generation**, not necessarily physical blade motion. DOE recommends particular turbine power curves and site/hub wind characterization for energy estimates, and says local wind can vary over short distances.
- [Doosan-authored WinDS3000 technical sheet (PDF copy)](https://www.visiongroup21.eu/en/pdf/tech6c.pdf): older 91.3 m WinDS3000 specification gives **3 MW, cut-in 3 m/s, rated wind 13 m/s, cut-out 25 m/s, rated rotor speed 15.7 RPM, 80 m hub** and a plotted electrical power curve. This is manufacturer-authored content hosted by a third party, **not a verified Tamra as-built/SCADA record**. Operator diameter 91.59 m differs slightly from this sheet's 91.3 m; prefer operator diameter in local estimates. The plotted curve is not tabulated, so do not invent exact points.
- [Doosan 2022 manufacturer brochure](https://www.doosanenerbility.com/heavy_file/business/data/wind_energy_vision/pdf_products_energy_Wind_Power_Brochure_Eng%282022%29.pdf): chronology names WinDS3000/91 and Tamra 2017 commissioning separately. [Doosan current lineup](https://www.doosanenerbility.com/en/business/wind_energy_lineup) lists a **134 m** WinDS3000 with 3/10/20 m/s cut-in/rated/cut-out. Those current 134 m figures **must not** be assigned to the older Tamra 91 m rotor.
- [NREL variable-speed wind turbine controls report](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy04osti/35816.pdf): below rated, a variable-speed turbine can vary rotor speed to maintain an efficient tip-speed ratio; at/above rated, pitch and controls hold power and rotor speed rather than allowing speed to grow linearly with wind. [DOE/NREL turbine model report](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/water/pdfs/39485.pdf) defines tip-speed ratio `λ = ωR/v` and shows `Cp` also depends on pitch; actual RPM requires a control law and operating state.

## Smallest honest display estimator

Use the nearby station reading only when the existing freshness/validity check passes. Show **"풍속 기반 추정"** for *indicative available electrical power*, never actual generation. If the exact Doosan power curve can be reliably digitized and Tamra's installed variant corroborated, interpolate that curve. Until then an expressly illustrative curve can use `P_est(v) = 0` below assumed `v_ci=3`; `P_est(v) = 3000 kW × (v³−3³)/(13³−3³)` for `3≤v<13`; `P_est(v)=3000 kW` for `13≤v<25`; and `P_est(v)=0` above assumed `25`, with the values marked as **generic WinDS3000/91 brochure assumptions**, not site settings. Clamp to `[0,3000]`. The cubic ramp is a visualization heuristic, not the manufacturer electrical power curve. In particular, do not multiply by ten and label it observed farm generation.

For motion, if using the same brochure assumptions, derive the **visual** below-rated rotor speed from constant tip-speed ratio calibrated to the brochure rated point: `RPM_est = min(15.7, 15.7 × v / 13)` for `3≤v<25`; use 0 visual RPM below cut-in and at/above cut-out, and hold approximately 15.7 between rated and cut-out. Equivalent `λ≈(15.7×2π/60)×(91.59/2)/13≈5.8`. This is plausible **partial-load animation**, not an actual RPM estimate; minimum rotor speed, start/stop delays, control hysteresis and coasting are unknown. A smooth displayed acceleration/deceleration would be visual polish only. 4 RPM constant demo should not remain labeled as physical rotation if this mode replaces it.

Wind direction can orient the nacelle toward the observed **wind-from** sector; it does not by itself determine power or RPM. Do not apply a yaw-loss factor without measured nacelle yaw/yaw error. The 16-point direction has ±11.25° quantization even before location/time differences. Ground-station wind is not the rotor-disc inflow; unknown offshore shear, turbulence, wake, density, turbine availability, curtailment and control state can materially change both power and RPM. A ten-minute *mean* cubed is not the ten-minute mean of cubed instantaneous wind speed. If displaying energy, `P_est/6` kWh for a ten-minute bin is only a coarse scenario under a constant-power assumption; do not call it metered production.
